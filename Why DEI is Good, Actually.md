But that’s exactly why DEI is a contentious issue, I think: It has been reduced and boiled down until it’s burned on the proverbial pan. No one really knows what it means, and when that blackened mess adorns their perfectly roasted chicken, they cry foul. On the part of American conservatives, I have heard (straight from the horse’s mouth) that DEI is an ideological litmus test, reverse discrimination, and undermines good-old American “meritocracy.” I naturally mistrust conservative characterizations of identity politics-based issues, I will concede that, but to the majority of the Republican population who are [mostly blue collar workers](https://www.zippia.com/advice/democratic-vs-republican-jobs/), DEI is mostly foreign to them; they simply lack the hands-on experience with its reasons for existence, mechanisms, and actual outcomes to form an educated opinion. So I think my burden of proof that DEI is “discriminatory against white men” needs more than the classic paltry anecdotal evidence \+ rhetoric combo that the right goes gaga for (see: [The Laken Riley Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/5))[^1]. 

It added to my skepticism (OK, let’s be honest, it added to my disgust) when DEI became a dog whistle for “people of color who liberal-ed themselves into decision-making positions.” No one batted an eye, I noticed. Do you know about the [glass cliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_cliff?)? I’ll summarize: women and POC, empirically, do not receive objective evaluations in leadership, and are commonly placed in leadership positions during periods of crisis or downturn when the risk of failure is the highest. ## The (Meritocratic) Merit of DEI

The main argument against DEI is its purported “preference” for historically marginalized groups over white men. Those who feel threatened by DEI hiring practices believe that organizations that implement it distort merit-based hiring and hire people, for example, “just because they’re black.” That’s a major oversimplification. In practice, it may lead to less white people being hired, but not because there’s a preference for hiring black people. To insert my own oversimplification, they’re merely getting rid of their preference for hiring white people. 

A true meritocracy is a system wherein individuals hold power based on their innate ability rather than social connections or status, a system that conservatives most definitely proselytize as the ideal. Let’s say DEI was out of the picture: Is corporate America a meritocracy in really any sense of the word? Absolutely not, and if you think it is, I have a bridge to sell you. 

I hope we can all agree that any kind of identity-based bias (whether on the basis of being white or black, gay or straight, disabled or non-disabled, etc.) does not serve a meritocratic society, nor does nepotism, or hiring on the basis of knowing someone in the company, or knowing someone who knows someone in the company. (As an almost-new grad, I have been told again and again and again that the way to get hired is to exploit a personal connection rather than apply straight-up.) If we agree, then the **actual, real, and true hiring practices employed by DEI initiatives contribute towards a more meritocratic society.** I’ll explain, because these are the most commonly used among organizations:

1. **Blind recruitment practices:** Names and demographic information are removed from resumes, aiming to [reduce bias](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366128032_Is_Blind_Recruitment_an_Effective_Recruitment_Method) (bias, by the way, that is horrifyingly [common](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2019/jan/17/minority-ethnic-britons-face-shocking-job-discrimination)).  
2. **Standardized, behavior-based interviews:** Instead of the free-form, conversation-esque interview-style, questions are consistent, related to skills, competencies, and behaviors, and then scored on a rubric. These techniques are aimed to reduce variance in responses (allowing for a clearer view of the interviewee’s actual skill set in relation to everyone else) and fairness in response quality assessment. This practice has been proven to [reduce confirmation bias](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36222998/).   
3. **Neutral-ifying job descriptions:** [Research shows](https://gender-decoder.katmatfield.com/static/documents/Gaucher-Friesen-Kay-JPSP-Gendered-Wording-in-Job-ads.pdf) that specific wording in job descriptions discourages women from applying. By eliminating that gendered language from the job description, there is a more representatively qualified group of applicants (and therefore more equitable).   
4. **Making an effort to advertise the job to marginalized groups:** Some companies will partner with minority-serving institutions or professional organizations to make sure that people from underrepresented groups apply, making the group of applicants more representative and diverse.

Now with these last couple of points you may be asking: “What is the actual benefit of having an applicant pool that is representative of the general population? Hell, what’s the benefit of a diverse pool of applicants other than the fact that they can now virtue signal about how progressive they are while having an easy excuse to shut out white people from the whole process?\! What’s the point of being equitable if it means that I have less opportunity?\!\!\!” Calm down, I say. Calm down. Take a deep breath. You’re going to be fine. 

The point of DEI initiatives is not to lower hiring standards, but **increase the pool of applicants** and ensure that merit is the primary consideration. It logically follows that this will include reducing bias. The perceived reduction in white hires is not a hostile takeover, it is a correction to a [historic overrepresentation](https://cepr.net/publications/the-continuing-power-of-white-preferences-in-employment/). To succeed, you need to be better than your peers, that much we can agree on. Now, unfortunately for us white dudes, the fight is more fair.[^4] 

## There Is A Real Economic Benefit To Being Diverse

Gasp. Diversity is *not* just a scam to make yourself more appealing to your left-leaning audience? The horror. There should be nothing surprising about this fact: Diversity is directly and quantifiably responsible for greater profits and productivity. Companies follow the money (and if they can cash in on easy PR points, they’ll do that too). Some rapid-fire facts: 

* Companies with diverse hiring practices are [35% more likely](https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/people%20and%20organizational%20performance/our%20insights/why%20diversity%20matters/diversity%20matters.pdf) to have financial returns higher than their respective national industry medians.  
* Diverse teams outperform individual decision-makers [87% of the time](https://www.peoplemanagement.co.uk/article/1742040/diversity-drives-better-decisions).  
* Diverse companies are [70% more likely](https://hbr.org/2013/12/how-diversity-can-drive-innovation) to capture new markets.  
* Companies with above-average diversity on their management teams report [19% higher](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2018/how-diverse-leadership-teams-boost-innovation) innovation revenue. 

So while we can all turn our collective noses at the blatantly performative “We’re so gay\! We’re so diverse\!” corporate messaging, there is a practicality behind it that goes beyond trying to score easy reputation points. Companies like Salesforce, Google, or Microsoft have successful DEI stories: it led to increased productivity, psychological safety, innovation, to name just a few positives. Feel free to look it up; they were very happy to tell us all about it so they could cash in said PR-bux.

## People In The Workforce Actually Care About Diversity

The hiring goal of any organization is to attract and retain top talent. Top talent, by and large, [values diverse and inclusive work environments](https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/glassdoors-diversity-and-inclusion-workplace-survey/). So companies are incentivized to build diverse and inclusive work environments. We could leave it at that (and remove a fair bit of snark) and have a fairly convincing case for DEI. 

## DEI Is Not Incompatible With Conservative Ideals

The basic principles of DEI—that is to say, fairness, inclusivity, and opportunity—are not, in theory, at odds with the conservative ethos. I would argue (in fact, I would *insist*) that the reason why conservatives so fervently dislike the concept of DEI is that it has been distorted and demonized in such a way, combined with examples of DEI-gone-wrong whether valid or completely untrue, that to say DEI in some spaces is comparable to saying “anti-white.” The main contributor to this effect is, in my view, a combination of cherry-picking (because they have no statistics or real data to show DEI is bad), and a cognitive distortion that I will henceforth define as the “Perverse Incentive Fallacy”: 

*Perverse Incentive Fallacy:  A reasoning error in which, when evaluating the value of some concept or idea, an individual mistakes the perverse incentives of the concept as its only end result, leading them to dismiss the concept or idea outright without considering its broader benefits or other mechanisms that mitigate said perverse incentives.*

Here is the linear chain of events I have seen conservatives claim occurs (and is certainly possible, and certainly has happened before, but I have seen **zero** evidence that this is the pervading scenario):

1\. “Woke” individuals gain power over hiring processes and diversity training.  
2\. Bias *against* whites is integrated into the hiring process and company culture, due to social justice wackjob nonsense about how whites are privileged and now it’s black peoples’ turn\!  
3\.  Naive leftists see a diverse workforce and virtue signaling about being “for the marginalized people” and celebrate, without seeing the clearly unjust side of the coin at all.

No wonder they think liberals are stupid. Everything is dumbed down and spoon-fed to them by whatever [Russian agent[^5]](https://www.nytimes.com/2022/04/15/technology/russia-media-fox-news.html) is currently clocked in, and those with half of a brain can see that there something isn’t quite right about it. Instead of looking in, however, their eyes narrow forever outward, scanning an empty horizon. Must be depressing.
